Abstract. Developing resilient and robust facility layouts is a long-term strategic problem 
for contemporary manufacturing systems, particularly in settings with operational uncer -
tainty and spatial complexity. When departments are assigned to multiple layout blocks of 
different area requirements and material flows are prone to disruption, conventional deter -
ministic models are not able to provide robust solutions. This paper bridges this gap by 
introducing a scenario-based robust facility layout model as a mixed-integer linear pro -
gramming (MILP) problem. We introduce an MILP formulation that couples the unequal- 
area facility layout problem with a capacitated network flow problem. Unlike standard 
models that assume direct paths, this approach explicitly models interblock logistics 
through capacity-constrained gateways (ramps). The model employs a penalty-based 
robust objective function that integrates expected cost minimization with stability devia -
tions and soft capacity constraints, allowing the identification of feasible solutions even 
under compound disruption scenarios where rigid models would fail. The proposed 
approach is demonstrated on a real-life application case from a leading European home 
appliance manufacturer based in Turkey, where the plant layout must fit changing opera -
tional conditions and capacity restrictions. Leveraging actual production data and expert 
knowledge such as observations of material surges and production shutdowns, realistic 
disruption scenarios are defined for the model to develop solutions that remain feasible 
and efficient in different operating states. Simulation-based verification shows that opti -
mized layouts preserve robust performance under dynamic and uncertain flow conditions. 
The proposed layout reduced total material handling distance by 19.3% and increased pro -
duction area utilization by 31% compared with the current layout. The study provides a 
scalable and data-driven approach to industrial facility planning and demonstrates the 
practical applicability of robust optimization to increase resilience under uncertainty in 
production settings.

Introduction
The facility layout problem (FLP) is a classical area of 
research in industrial engineering and operations 
research that has a direct influence on the performance 
of manufacturing systems. FLP is concerned with find -
ing the most appropriate location for departments, 
machines, or workstations in a facility to reduce material 
handling costs and improve overall production perfor -
mance (Deshpande et al. 2016, Sun et al. 2018). An effec -
tive layout eliminates unnecessary movement, improves 
production capacity, minimizes energy consumption 
and resource utilization, and ultimately reduces opera -
tional costs (Ku et al. 2011, Navidi et al. 2012). In contrast, 
ineffective layouts may result in higher transportation 
costs and lower production capacity, eventually increas -
ing production costs. Even a small improvement in lay -
out design can bring about quantifiable gains.
Previously, the majority of FLP research made 
simplifying assumptions, for example, equally sized 
departments or deterministic flow matrices. More recent 
work shows that such assumptions do not reflect the 
complexity of operational processes in contemporary 
manufacturing settings. In practice, departments are dif -
ferent in shape and size and have to meet a variety of 
operational constraints (Loiola et al. 2007, Grobelny and 
Michalski 2017). In order to capture these complexities,

recent research has considered the unequal-area FLP 
(UA-FLP), trying to locate departments of varying 
dimensions in constrained space such that nonoverlap 
and realistic placement are guaranteed (Drira et al.

Furthermore, the feasibility of the layout is also influ -
enced by physical constraints, such as fixed infrastruc -
ture, safety clearances, and transportation pathways. 
Such factors are of prime importance in real-world 
environments and can play a major role in influencing 
material routing and department location. For example, 
obstacles in the form of walls or pillars can limit viable 
solutions and make internal logistics more complex, 
especially when material handling is vehicle-based 
using forklifts or automated guided vehicles (AGVs) 
(Besbes et al. 2020 ). Along with these complexities, real 
production environments experience high operational 
variability. Remanufacturing environments, for exam -
ple, experience significant processing-time variability 
due to uncertainty in core quality and condition, as dem -
onstrated by Kekre et al. ( 2003 ). Material flows among 
departments may vary according to the type of product, 
season, or availability of resources, whereas dynamic 
layout models attempt to modify and evolve over time. 
However, such reconfigurations are often expensive and 
disruptive, leading to production downtime (Anjos and 
Vieira 2017 , Hosseini-Nasab et al. 2018 , Kikolski et al. 
2018 ). This challenge has been observed in studies of 
next-generation factory layouts. Benjaafar et al. ( 2002 ) 
argued that facilities need layouts designed for robust -
ness when both production uncertainty and relayout 
costs are high, rather than layouts optimized for a single 
set of conditions. Instead of frequent time-based modifi -
cations, this research aims to create a layout that main -
tains consistent performance across a set of predefined 
disruption scenarios. The physical layout remains 
fixed, whereas flow routing and ramp selection adapt 
to each scenario. This scenario-based strategy enables 
long-term planning through the capture of essential 
operational variations without demanding repeated 
physical reconfigurations.
Uncertainty is a key issue in FLP modeling. Material 
flows in the real world vary based on seasonality, equip -
ment breakdowns, scheduling changes, and supply 
unpredictability. Home appliance manufacturing indus -
tries that handle various product lines, process depen -
dencies, and multimodal transport type are primarily 
subject to such uncertainty (Derakhshan Asl and Wong 
2017 ). Disregarding these layout inconsistencies can 
lead to inefficient or unrealistic designs when applied to 
real-world environments. There have been some 
attempts to account for uncertainty through probabilis -
tic, interval-based, or scenario-based approaches. For 
example, Peng et al. ( 2018 ) dealt with stochastic flows, 
and Yazdani et al. ( 2023 ) suggested robust optimization 
models based on uncertainty scenarios. Most of these 
models provide theoretical contributions but are not 
applied in practice. They tend to employ hypothetical 
scenarios and omit essential aspects or operational 
details. Moreover, few have used historical production 
data to estimate uncertainty or examine the robustness 
of the layout under real disruptions.
To fill these gaps, this article proposes a scenario-based 
robust optimization model that advances the standard 
UA-FLP by incorporating a simultaneous routing and 
layout logic. The model is a mixed-integer linear program 
(MILP) formulation that treats interblock connections not 
as infinite-capacity links, but as capacity-constrained bot -
tlenecks (ramps) whose availability varies by scenario. By 
integrating soft constraints for capacity violations, the 
model prioritizes operational feasibility under stress con -
ditions, a critical feature for industrial realism often miss -
ing in rigid academic formulations.
The proposed approach is validated through a case 
study of a leading European home appliance manufac -
turer. We focused on the washing machine plant, where 
departments such as plastic injection, metal forming, 
painting, and final assembly are physically distributed 
among blocks. Material flow between blocks occurs 
through designated ramps, subject to capacity and avail -
ability constraints.
The main contributions of this research are threefold. 
• Methodological Integration of Layout and Rout -
ing: We present a formulation that solves the facility 
layout problem and the interblock network flow prob -
lem simultaneously. This allows the model to optimize 
department locations based on the availability and 
capacity of specific transport ramps across different 
scenarios, providing a higher degree of realism than 
distance-based metrics alone.
• Feasibility-Robust Formulation: Unlike tradi -
tional robust models that may become infeasible under 
severe disruptions, our model incorporates a penalty- 
based soft constraint mechanism. This allows the sys -
tem to identify the “least-disruptive” feasible layout 
even when scenarios exceed nominal design capacities 
(e.g., during combined ramp failures).
• Data-Driven Validation Framework: We demon -
strate a systematic framework for converting raw his -
torical maintenance and production logs into discrete 
disruption scenarios. This bridges the gap between the -
oretical uncertainty modeling and practical industrial 
data availability, validated via simulation to quantify 
transport efficiency gains.

Model section presents the mathematical framework. 
The application section explains the case study and 
development of the scenario. The Results section reports 
the computational results and findings. Finally, the Con -
clusion section concludes the paper and suggests future 
research.

Related Literature
Facility layout design is a classic industrial engineering 
problem that deals with the arrangement of depart -
ments, machines, or workstations to enhance efficiency 
and minimize material handling costs. Classical models 
generally presume static layouts, deterministic data, and 
single-floor settings. The increased sophistication of con -
temporary manufacturing—marked by flexible systems, 
dynamic flows, and environmental concerns—has 
prompted researchers to examine more realistic pro -
blems with dynamic, uncertain, and multifloor settings. 
These have led to new modeling directions including 
dynamic facility layout problems, robust and stochastic 
formulations, and multiobjective models addressing 
cost, energy, and space utilization.
In the literature, several papers have reviewed devel -
opments on the facility layout problem. Drira et al. ( 2007 ) 
typified layout issues by space configurations, decision 
variables, and solution approaches. Ahmadi et al. ( 2017 ) 
highlighted modeling complications of multifloor set -
tings, including elevator design and interfloor flows. 
P ´erez-Gosende et al. ( 2021 ) surveyed the growing appli -
cation of metaheuristics and hybrid optimization techni -
ques in practical layouts. Building on these foundational 
reviews, this paper focuses on optimization-based, robust 
and dynamic, simulation-supported, and application- 
specific facility layout models. Special attention is paid to 
research addressing uncertainty, accommodating opera -
tional variability, and striving for practical applicability in 
complicated manufacturing systems.
Optimization-Based and Metaheuristic Methods
A significant portion of the FLP literature has concentrated 
on optimization techniques, both mathematical program -
ming and metaheuristics. These techniques address 
increasingly difficult layout issues—like unequal-area 
departments, energy-efficient layouts, multifloor layouts, 
or dynamic redesigns—while enhancing efficiency, reduc -
ing costs, or optimizing space.
Numerous studies have applied mathematical model -
ing and hybrid heuristics to enhance layout decision- 
making. Dorrah and Marzouk ( 2021 ) combined agent- 
based modeling, queuing theory, and a multiobjective 
assignment model for administrative facilities. Zhang 
and Lin ( 2024 ) applied multiagent reinforcement learn -
ing with 3D A* search in connector assembly, whereas 
Subulan et al. ( 2023 ) proposed a heuristic decomposition 
model including machine capabilities for the UA-FLP. 
Other metaheuristics include learning-based simulated 
annealing (Lin et al. 2024 ), a modified Non-dominated 
Sorting Genetic Algorithm II for an air conditioner plant 
(Guo et al. 2023 ), and teaching-learning–based optimiza -
tion for through-aisle layouts (Zhang et al. 2024 ).
Recent studies have incorporated sustainability and 
energy efficiency into facility layout modeling. Zhang 
et al. ( 2022 ) proposed an energy-conscious model using 
multiobjective particle swarm optimization, and Peron 
et al. ( 2020 ) developed dynamic layout planning via 
three-dimensional (3D) mapping and motion capture. 
Mayadunne et al. ( 2024 ) presented a two-stage MILP for 
warehouse storage efficiency and bin sizing. Other niche 
methods include Monarch butterfly optimization for 
AGV-based single-loop layouts (Kim and Chae 2019 ), 
discrete multiobjective particle swarm optimization in 
multiworkshop settings (Guan et al. 2019 ), and differen -
tial evolution in double-row layouts (Ji et al. 2023 ).
Reinforcement learning and hybrid metaheuristics 
have been considered for their flexibility under uncer -
tainty. Kaven et al. ( 2024 ) utilized multiagent reinforce -
ment learning with proximal policy optimization in 
flexible layouts, and Klar et al. ( 2022 ) demonstrated 
enhancements in large-scale layouts with double deep Q 
network. Configuration-space and slicing-based encod -
ings were developed in Liu et al. ( 2020 ) and La Scalia 
et al. ( 2019 ). Fuzzy programming and robust models 
were presented in Kaveh et al. ( 2014 ) and Saeidi ( 2024 ), 
whereas simulation-supported optimization appeared 
in Emami and Nookabadi ( 2013 ) and Jolai et al. ( 2012 ). 
Fuzzy models combined with metaheuristics were used 
in Abedzadeh et al. ( 2013 ), Zhun et al. ( 2021 ), and Besbes 
et al. ( 2021 ). Layout models were also extended with 
domain-specific constraints and multiobjective perfor -
mance, such as double-row MILPs with valid inequalities 
(Amaral 2024 ), I/O- constrained UA-FLPs (Muhayat and 
Utamima 2024 ), and mixed-integer nonlinear formula -
tions (Seyedi et al. 2024 ). Multifloor or multiobjective lay -
outs were studied using ant colony (Liu and Liu 2019 ) or 
permutation-based Genetic Algorithms (GAs) (Kalita and 
Datta 2024 ). Other contributions include robust layout 
enhancements by power values with normal search 
Abedzadeh et al. ( 2013 ), hybrid simulated annealing 
approaches (Matai 2015 , Oktaviani and Utamima 2024 ), 
and simulation-integrated demand forecasting using 
long short-term memory (Shoushtari et al. 2024 ). Collec -
tively, these works show that metaheuristic and mathe -
matical models continue to advance toward more 
realistic layout problems. Nonetheless, they generally do 
not address scenario-based robustness, flow uncertainty, 
or spatial constraints like interblock routing and ramp 
limitations, which this research seeks to address.
Dynamic and Robust Layout Models
Robust manufacturing layouts are needed to remain 
effective under unforeseen conditions such as fluctuat -
ing demand, supply disruptions, or shifting product 
flows. Frequent reconfigurations are costly, and disrup -
tive, which motivates the use of strong and flexible 
facility layout models that change internally and main -
tain performance across multiple scenarios. A classifica -
tion framework for layout design based on production 
uncertainty and relayout cost was developed by Benjaafar

Several studies have implemented scenario-based

physical reconfigurations. Similarly, Cheng et al. ( 2021 )

for disruption risk mitigation. Xiao et al. ( 2019 ) opti -

particle swarm optimization to reduce rearrangement 
costs. In the healthcare sector, Tongur et al. ( 2020 ) dem -

Multilevel and spatial issues were considered by Iza -

Stochastic demand was handled by Moslemipour et al.

dynamic facility layout design to enhance dependability 
under variable demands.
Although these works extensively advance layout 
modeling, none combine scenario-based robustness 
with interblock routing and spatial limitations like ramp 
capacities—a gap this paper addresses. By creating a 
scenario-based robust MILP model with these realistic 
industrial operating constraints, this work closes the gap 
between theoretical research and practical industrial 
applications in layout design under uncertainty.
Simulation-Based Approaches
Simulation approaches have become increasingly valuable 
for facility layout design under uncertain and variable con -
ditions. Combining simulation with optimization, machine 
learning, or reinforcement learning allows scholars to eval -
uate layout performance under varying conditions and 
enhance decision-making flexibility.
Recent studies have explored simulation-integrated 
layout design using advanced algorithms and hybrid 
frameworks. For instance, Bi et al. ( 2024 ) proposed an 
optimization approach for workshop layouts using the 
sparrow search algorithm (SSA), aiming to reduce trans -
portation costs and improve nonlogistic relationships. 
Their results, verified via FlexSim, showed that SSA pro -
vided stable and optimal solutions. Likewise, Klar et al. 
( 2024 ) developed a transferable framework for multiob -
jective layout planning using discrete event simulation 
and deep reinforcement learning, which proved efficient 
for uncertain planning with transfer learning. In another 
study, Z ´u ˜niga et al. ( 2020 ) introduced a five-phase 
simulation and optimization framework for redesigning 
facility layouts, leading to more usable and resilient 
arrangements in industrial cases. Choi and Kim ( 2024 ) 
proposed an intelligent arrangement system combining 
MILP, simulation-based optimization, and digital twin 
technologies to refine layouts and reduce time and effort 
for efficient design.
Modeling realistic spatial limitations, Besbes et al. 
( 2020 ) integrated the A* pathfinding algorithm with 
simulation and a genetic algorithm for more real- 
world-like arrangements, introducing movement logic 
into layout decision making. In modular construction, 
Yang and Lu ( 2023 ) contrasted arrangement configura -
tions using simulation-based optimization and revealed 
cost-productivity tradeoffs. Safety was considered by 
Vasudevan and Son ( 2011 ), who used a multiparadigm 
simulation framework to analyze productivity and emer -
gency evacuation, demonstrating that some layouts could 
achieve both. Lee ( 2012 ) combined an MILP model with 
simulation logic and preprocessing to improve computa -
tional efficiency and solution quality. These studies dem -
onstrate that simulation enables robust layout assessment 
under uncertainty, spatial constraints, and time-varying 
flow, and its integration with optimization and learning 
enhances flexibility for industrial applications. A sum -
mary of robust facility location papers is provided in 
Table 1 .

have shown promise for facility layout optimization, 
this research employs MILP because it naturally accom -
modates the multiscenario structure of the problem.

Although the facility layout problem has been studied 
in detail, there are still important gaps. Most models 
focus on specific issues such as unequal-area depart -
ments, changing demand, or uncertainty, but few com -
bine these in a clear and practical way. Very few 
consider more than one spatial or operational constraint, 
such as interblock flows or low-capacity ramps. 
Scenario-based models are becoming more common but 
are often limited to simplified cases and are rarely tested 
with real manufacturing data. Models that consider flow 
changes or space constraints often cannot scale to large 
systems or provide practical solutions.
To address these problems, we present a scenario- 
based facility layout model designed to reflect realistic 
operating conditions. Using real flow and layout data 
from a home appliance factory, the model is solved across 
multiple disruption scenarios based on historical records. 
Although scenario-based robust optimization for facility 
layout has gained attention in recent literature, system
-
atic scenario generation from disaggregated historical 
production data remains underexplored. Our approach 
extracts disruption characteristics—including failure fre
-
quencies, demand variations, and capacity constraints— 
from three years of operational records combined with 
expert validation, grounding the optimization model in 
observed patterns rather than theoretical assumptions. It 
also includes spatial constraints like interblock ramp lim
-
its and additional cost effects. By solving one large MILP 
model for all scenarios at once, the approach provides 
layout solutions that work well in normal conditions and 
remain reliable during disruptions. This study offers a 
practical and robust tool to support layout planning in 
real manufacturing environments.
Mathematical Model
This section presents the single-period robust facility 
layout model to address uncertainty in flow volumes, 
transportation costs, and ramp capacities. The model 
makes two types of decisions: strategic design decisions 
(department locations and block assignments) that 
remain permanently fixed across all scenarios and tacti -
cal operational decisions (flow routing and ramp usage) 
that adapt internally to each scenario’s conditions with -
out requiring physical reconfiguration. This formulation 
is appropriate for greenfield facility design or major 
brownfield redesign projects where the spatial layout 
represents a long-term commitment, and operational 
flexibility is achieved through intelligent routing rather 
than costly physical rearrangements.
The objective function includes three components: (i) 
expected transportation cost across scenarios; (ii) devia -
tion penalties to ensure layout stability; and (iii) soft pen -
alties for violations such as ramp capacity overruns. 
Two parameters regulate the tradeoffs: the deviation 
penalty weight controls how much the model values 
cost stability versus expected cost, whereas the violation 
penalty weight represents the cost of capacity overruns 
(e.g., overtime, expedited handling, or temporary capac
-
ity expansion). This formulation ensures that the result -
ing layout is not only cost-effective under expected 
conditions but also remains robust and functional when 
disruptions occur, avoiding designs that perform well 
on average but fail catastrophically in certain scenarios.
Transportation costs include internal movement 
within blocks (based on Manhattan distances and flow 
volumes), external transfers between blocks via ramps 
(based on ramp-to-ramp distances and scenario-specific 
cost factors), and closeness penalties for separating 
departments that should remain adjacent due to work
-
flow or operational requirements. The model enforces 
several operational constraints that vary by scenario. 
Material flows between departments in different blocks 
must be routed through designated ramp pairs, whereas 
flows within the same block incur only internal transport 
costs. Each ramp has a capacity limit that can be 
exceeded at a penalty cost, allowing the model to find 
feasible solutions even when all scenarios cannot be 
accommodated within strict capacity bounds. The model 
tracks which ramps are actually used for each flow path, 
ensuring that routing decisions reflect real material han -
dling requirements rather than artificial shortcuts.
The model ensures geometric feasibility through sev -
eral spatial constraints. Departments within the same 
block are positioned such that they do not overlap, with

Application: Real-World Facility Layout at 
a Home Appliance Plant
The suggested scenario-based robust facility layout 
design model is implemented in production plant of one 
of the largest producers of home appliances in Europe. 
The factory manufactures various products such as 
refrigerators, dishwashers, washing machines, dryers, 
ovens, and cooling units. The plant is a high-volume 
facility with stringent efficiency demands and contains 
many departments performing metal forming, coating, 
subassembly, final assembly, and testing. These depart -
ments are spread over physically distinct layout blocks, 
and hence the design issue is especially difficult because 
of interblock material flows and space limitations.
A prominent feature of the facility is that it possesses 
nonuniform departmental area requirements and a 
dynamic production environment. Departments such as 
plastic injection and metal forming require large floor 
areas for heavy machinery, whereas final assembly and 
testing stations occupy smaller, more flexible spaces. Pro -
duction scheduling must simultaneously accommodate 
multiple washing machine models with different proces -
sing sequences, creating competing demands for shared 
painting lines and material handling capacity. Not only 
are material flows between departments high density and 
high volume, but they are also sensitive to demand pat -
tern changes, product mix variations, and sensitive to 
operational disruptions. The presence of multiple ramps 
connecting blocks further complicates the routing options 
under varying capacity and cost considerations. All these 
factors make the facility an excellent application case for 
the study of robust and flexible layout designs.
To represent realistic operating complexity, the layout 
model considers multiple future scenarios instead of one 
nominal or worst-case flow matrix. The scenarios repre -
sent possible variations in internal flows, external trans -
port distances, and ramp availability. Each scenario is 
generated from historical production data and domain 
knowledge and is assigned a probability to represent its 
likelihood. The formulation enables the model to deter -
mine a layout that is feasible and cost-effective for a broad 
range of operating conditions, representing realistic tra -
deoffs between efficiency, stability, and robustness.
Scenario Definition and Disruption Modeling
In order to assess the proposed layout’s performance in 
the face of uncertainty, 10 discrete scenarios were estab -
lished, each of which corresponds to a conceivable future 
state of the production system. The scenarios were cre -

The scenarios considered in this study were defined 
after a detailed analysis of historical production data 
and consultation with production experts. The data are 
composed of disaggregated daily and shift-level infor -
mation for material flows among departments, record -
ing seasonal fluctuations, annual growth, planned 
shutdowns, and statutory holidays. We reviewed these 
data to identify normal patterns and rare events, such as 
sudden increases in demand, slowdowns, supply inter -
ruptions, and ramp failures. For events that were not fre -
quent in the observed data, their frequency and impact 
were defined based on maintenance records and expert 
judgment. In this way, each scenario in the model has 
either direct empirical support from the operational data 
or the practical experience of specialists in the 
manufacturing environment. This ensures that the test -
ing of layout performance is conducted with realistic 
scenarios and covers the major sources of uncertainty 
faced by decision makers. The definition of these scenar -
ios involved an iterative process. Initial model runs 
based purely on theoretical failure rates were reviewed 
by plant managers; based on their feedback, the proba -
bility and impact of the Combined Stress scenario were 
refined to better align with the frequency of actual his -
torical maintenance incidents, ensuring the final results 
represented actionable operational risks.
Ten disruption scenarios were formulated from expert 
opinion and historical data to capture a variety of real- 
world operating conditions. Scenario 1 is the base case 
with nominal flow volumes and all ramps available 
(25%). Scenario 2 assumes a moderate escalation of criti -
cal flows (peak demand, 15%), and Scenario 3 simulates a 
uniform decrease in total flow (low demand, 15%). Sce -
nario 4 models the failure of one ramp (ramp 2 failure, 
10%), and Scenario 5 assumes decreased output in 
selected key departments (production disruption, 8%).

Conclusions
This research presented a scenario-based robust 
facility layout model to tackle the uncertainty and 
operational variability that are commonly found in 
real-world manufacturing practice. In contrast to 
deterministic models that optimize the layout with 
respect to a single nominal assumption, the proposed 
model integrated 10 disruption scenarios, each reflect
-
ing a realistic operational condition. Scenario probabil -
ities were introduced to reflect the likelihood of every

layout in a broad spectrum of future states. Joint opti -
mization of department placement, flow routing, and

The results confirmed that the proposed layout 
effectively balanced spatial efficiency, transport cost, 
and robustness. Departments with high flow intensity 
were placed in such a manner as to restrict interblock 
traffic, and routing flexibility across a range of ramp 
types created the capacity to respond to stress without 
appreciable loss of performance. Although the average 
utilization of the ramps was within capacity, the 
scenario-based analysis revealed how stress condi -
tions would create overloads on specific ramps, parti -
cularly when the key infrastructure was out of service. 
The model handled such occurrences by intelligent 
redistribution of flows, achieving operational feasibil -
ity for most scenarios and minimizing violations when 
unavoidable.
Significantly, the model was not premised on fixed 
routing or inflexible assignments. Rather, the model 
evaluates how flow paths would be routed under differ -
ent disruption circumstances during the design phase, 
ensuring the layout remains feasible and efficient across 
operational scenarios. Scenario-by-scenario flow volume 
profiles also illustrated the model’s capacity to accom -
modate different demand levels and supply bottlenecks 
without sacrificing transport efficiency.
Cumulatively, this research offers a robust and resil -
ient solution to facility layout design under uncertainty. 
The model constitutes a rigorous decision support 
instrument for industrial planners that can anticipate 
and mitigate the impact of real-world disruptions. 
Through the combination of probabilistic modeling, 
ramp-level routing, and spatial constraints in a unified 
optimization framework, the study advances the state of 
layout planning in complex production systems. The 
major obstacles were in developing realistic disruption 
scenarios and managing computational intensity in solv -
ing a large-scale MILP. These were addressed through 
data preprocessing and careful scenario design so that 
the final model was still manageable but representative 
of industrial scenarios.
Future work can address integration into simula -
tion environments, digital twins, or real-time layout 
adjustment based on live production data. The results 
of this research form a basis for such extensions and 
demonstrate the practical applicability of robust lay -
out optimization in today’s dynamic manufacturing 
environment.
From a managerial perspective, the model provides a 
systematic yet adaptive decision support instrument 
that allows layout planners to actively assess the impact 
of various disruption scenarios—be it equipment break -
downs, capacity changes, or demand spikes—on spatial 
assignments, interblock routing, and ramp usage. Rather 
than responding to disruptions after they have occurred, 
managers are able to utilize the model to create facility
